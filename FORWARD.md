# Forward

4B pretrain train step. The network field is 256³ at 4 nm. Each row is the native spacing, ordered z × y × x. Read sizes are the voxel counts that cover that field: `round(256 × 4 / nm)`.

The SSL order is Read, Augment, Degrade, Corrupt, Missing. Augment on every row is one gamma for the whole crop (probability 0.5, gamma 0.8–1.25). That volume is the loss target, and it is the same along every axis. Degrade downsamples z of it on a 4 nm crop (`zf` 2–10); each slab is the mean of the 4 nm planes the nearest upsample later repeats it over, as a real section images its whole thickness. On an anisotropic crop it instead, with probability 0.8, downsamples one random in-plane axis the same way to the native z spacing, and the target keeps that axis fine; after the permutation that axis can point anywhere, so the loss needs real fine detail along it. The network gets no text prompt (null text); `{imaging} · ssl` is only logged to TensorBoard. Corrupt is per-section jitter (probability 0.5, ±2 voxels), per-section gamma (probability 0.5, 0.8–1.25), and added noise (probability 0.3, std 0.01), on the degraded input only. Missing runs last on every row: it zeroes whole native sections (probability 0.3, at most 2, fill zero), so each one is a whole blank slab on the 4 nm cube. After the forward pass, Resample averages the 4 nm raw head to the native resolution. Each native voxel is the mean of exactly the 4 nm voxels the nearest upsample copied it to, so windows never overlap, even at a fractional ratio. A 4 nm crop skips it.

On train, a random zyx permutation is applied after the degraded image is on the 4 nm cube. The augmented target is permuted with it, then nearest-upsampled, and Resample uses that permuted native resolution. Batch size stays 2. Validation skips the permutation. TensorBoard shows the 256³ cube on every row. The flow term runs only on crops whose native grid is the 4 nm cube; on coarser crops its clean endpoint would be the nearest-repeated slabs.
